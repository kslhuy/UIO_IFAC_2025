\title{Learning with Unknown Input Observers for Robust Nonlinear Estimation}

\begin{abstract}
This paper proposes a hybrid estimator for nonlinear systems affected by unknown dynamics. A regularized unknown input observer (UIO) first provides physics-consistent estimates of the state and unknown input, even when the standard rank condition is not directly satisfied. These estimates then supervise an online neural correction, yielding a teacher--student architecture that combines robustness and adaptability. The method is specialized to vehicle state estimation by separating tire-force residuals from remaining modeling errors. Compact LMI conditions certify the observer gains, while the vehicle case study shows how the UIO-generated labels are used for residual learning.
\end{abstract}

\begin{keyword}
Robust estimation; Unknown input observer; Model learning; Neural network; Vehicle dynamics.
\end{keyword}

\section{Introduction}\label{sec_introduction}

Reliable vehicle state estimation is difficult when the available sensors do not reveal all the forces acting on the vehicle. Tire-road friction changes, load transfer, biases, delays, and discretization errors can all enter the dynamics as unknown inputs. Physics-based observers give useful robustness guarantees, but classical unknown input observers require structural rank conditions that are often too restrictive for practical sensing configurations \citep{Hou:256351,Trinh:TAC08,Phanomchoeng:ASME/TM14}. Data-driven estimators can learn nonlinear mismatch, yet pure learning approaches provide limited guarantees during online adaptation \citep{2006Apdat,HO_GP}.

This paper combines both viewpoints. The first layer is a generalized UIO that reconstructs the state and an unknown input estimate. When the standard condition $\rank(CB)=\rank(B)$ is not available, the model is regularized so that the residual effect is explicitly bounded. The second layer is a neural observer trained by the UIO estimate rather than by an unavailable ground truth disturbance. The chapter result in \texttt{ch4\_main\_copy.tex} is merged here as a compact vehicle case study: the UIO teacher estimates tire-only residual forces, while the neural student learns the remaining mismatch from teacher-generated correction labels.

The contribution is threefold: (i) a regularized continuous-time UIO design with an $\mathcal H^1$ error bound; (ii) a UIO-supervised neural residual learner with an $\mathcal L_2$ observer design condition; and (iii) a vehicle implementation that separates tire residual reconstruction from data-driven correction.

\section{Regularized UIO Design}\label{sec_uio}

Consider the nonlinear system
\begin{equation}\label{sys_1}
    \dot{x}=\varphi(x,u)+B\mu_x,\qquad y=Cx,
\end{equation}
where $x\in\mathbb R^{n_x}$, $u\in\mathbb R^{n_u}$, $y\in\mathbb R^{n_y}$, and $\mu_x\in\mathbb R^{n_\mu}$ is an unknown nonlinear term collecting unmodeled dynamics and disturbances. The matrices $B$ and $C$ are known. We assume that $n_\mu\le n_y$, that $\varphi(\cdot,u)$ is Lipschitz on the operating set, and that $\mu_x$ is $\mathcal L_2$-bounded.

Direct unknown-input reconstruction usually requires
\begin{equation}\label{rank}
    \rank(CB)=\rank(B).
\end{equation}
To avoid losing feasibility when \eqref{rank} fails, we use the regularized descriptor representation
\begin{equation}\label{ali_syst1}
\begin{array}{l}
    \dot{x}+E_{\delta_1}\dot{\mu}_x=\varphi(x,u)+B\mu_x+E\omega_{\delta_1},\\
    y=Cx+D_{\delta_2}\mu_x+D\omega_{\delta_2},
\end{array}
\end{equation}
where $E_{\delta_1}=\delta_1E$, $D_{\delta_2}=\delta_2D$, $\omega_{\delta_1}=\delta_1\dot{\mu}_x$, and $\omega_{\delta_2}=\delta_2\mu_x$. The matrices $E$ and $D$ are selected so that, with
\begin{equation}\label{compact_descriptor}
\begin{gathered}
    \zeta=\begin{bmatrix}x\\ \mu_x\end{bmatrix},\quad
    \mathbb E=\begin{bmatrix}I_{n_x}&E_{\delta_1}\end{bmatrix},\quad
    \mathcal H=\begin{bmatrix}C&D_{\delta_2}\end{bmatrix},\\
    \bar E=\begin{bmatrix}E&0\end{bmatrix},\quad
    \bar D=\begin{bmatrix}0&D\end{bmatrix},\quad
    \bm\omega_\delta=\begin{bmatrix}\omega_{\delta_1}\\ \omega_{\delta_2}\end{bmatrix},
\end{gathered}
\end{equation}
the rank condition $\rank([\mathbb E^\top\ \mathcal H^\top]^\top)=n_x+n_\mu$ holds. Then \eqref{ali_syst1} becomes
\begin{equation}\label{descriptor}
    \mathbb E\dot{\zeta}=\varphi_\zeta(\zeta,u)+\bar E\bm\omega_\delta,\qquad
    y=\mathcal H\zeta+\bar D\bm\omega_\delta,
\end{equation}
with $\varphi_\zeta(\zeta,u)=\varphi(x,u)+B\mu_x$.

Let $P_\zeta$ and $Q_\zeta$ satisfy
\begin{equation}\label{peqh}
    P_\zeta\mathbb E+Q_\zeta\mathcal H=I_{n_x+n_\mu}.
\end{equation}
The observer is
\begin{equation}\label{uio_observer}
\dot{\eta}=P_\zeta\varphi_\zeta(\hat{\zeta},u)+K(y-\mathcal H\hat{\zeta}),\qquad
\hat{\zeta}=\eta+Q_\zeta y .
\end{equation}
Defining $\tilde{\zeta}=\zeta-\hat{\zeta}$ and $\xi=\tilde{\zeta}+Q_\zeta\bar D\bm\omega_\delta$, the error dynamics can be written as
\begin{equation}\label{xi_dyn}
\dot{\xi}=(P_\zeta A_j-K\mathcal H)\xi+
(\bar E-P_\zeta A_jQ_\zeta\bar D-K\mathcal D)\bm\omega_\delta
\end{equation}
for each vertex $A_j$ of a polytopic enclosure of the Jacobian of $\varphi_\zeta$, where $\mathcal D=(I+\mathcal H Q_\zeta)\bar D$.

\begin{theorem}\label{ali_thm}
If there exist $\mathcal P=\mathcal P^\top>0$, $\mathcal Q$, $\lambda>0$, and a fixed $\epsilon>0$ such that, for all Jacobian vertices $A_j$,
\begin{equation}\label{ali_LMI}
\begin{bmatrix}
\Phi_j & \mathcal P\mathcal E_j-\mathcal Q^\top\mathcal D & \Psi_j\\
\star & -\lambda I & \mathcal E_j^\top\mathcal P-\mathcal D^\top\mathcal Q\\
\star & \star & -\epsilon^{-1}\mathcal P
\end{bmatrix}<0,
\end{equation}
where
\[
\Phi_j=(P_\zeta A_j)^\top\mathcal P+\mathcal P(P_\zeta A_j)
-\mathcal H^\top\mathcal Q-\mathcal Q^\top\mathcal H,
\]
\[
\Psi_j=(P_\zeta A_j)^\top\mathcal P-\mathcal H^\top\mathcal Q,\qquad
\mathcal E_j=\bar E-P_\zeta A_jQ_\zeta\bar D,
\]
then the observer gain $K=\mathcal P^{-1}\mathcal Q^\top$ ensures
\begin{equation}\label{h1_bound}
    \|\tilde{\zeta}\|_{\mathcal H^1}\le
    \lambda_\delta\|\mu_x\|_{\mathcal H^1}+\beta\|\xi(0)\|.
\end{equation}
The constants $\lambda_\delta$ and $\beta$ are proportional to $\max(\delta_1,\delta_2)$ and $\sqrt{\lambda_{\max}(\mathcal P)}$, respectively.
\end{theorem}

\begin{pf}
With $V=\xi^\top\mathcal P\xi$ and $\mathcal S=\epsilon\mathcal P$, the Schur complement of \eqref{ali_LMI} gives
\[
\dot V+\|\xi\|^2+\dot{\xi}^{\top}\mathcal S\dot{\xi}
-\lambda\|\bm\omega_\delta\|^2\le0 .
\]
Integration on $[0,\infty)$ yields an $\mathcal H^1$ bound for $\xi$. Since $\tilde{\zeta}=\xi-Q_\zeta\bar D\bm\omega_\delta$ and $\|\bm\omega_\delta\|_{\mathcal L_2}\le\max(\delta_1,\delta_2)\|\mu_x\|_{\mathcal H^1}$, \eqref{h1_bound} follows.
\end{pf}

\section{Generalized Output-Derivative UIO}\label{output_derivative}

The previous regularization is useful when \eqref{rank} is not satisfied, but it may not give zero-error convergence. A less conservative construction augments the system with $\chi_1=y$ and $\chi_2=\dot y$:
\begin{equation}\label{gen_model}
\begin{array}{rcl}
\dot{\chi}_1&=&\chi_2,\\
\dot{\chi}_2&=&C\bar\varphi_x(x,u)+C\dfrac{\partial\varphi}{\partial x}(x,u)B\mu_x+CB\dot{\mu}_x,\\
\dot{x}&=&\varphi(x,u)+B\mu_x,\\
y_\chi&=&\chi_1+C_\chi\chi_2+Cx,
\end{array}
\end{equation}
where $\bar\varphi_x=(\partial\varphi/\partial x)\varphi$. Applying the same regularization to the $\dot{\chi}_2$ equation gives another descriptor model of the form \eqref{descriptor}, now with
\[
\zeta=\begin{bmatrix}\chi_1&\chi_2&x&\mu_x\end{bmatrix}^\top .
\]
The observer \eqref{uio_observer} and LMI \eqref{ali_LMI} apply directly after replacing the corresponding matrices. If $\rank(CB)=\rank(B)$, one can set $\delta_1=\delta_2=0$ with a suitable $C_\chi$, so $\bm\omega_\delta\equiv0$ and the error converges exponentially. If the rank condition fails, $\delta_1$ and $\delta_2$ tune the residual error bound. The auxiliary state $\hat{\chi}_2$ also provides a filtered estimate of $\dot y$, avoiding direct numerical differentiation of the measured output.

\section{UIO-Supervised Residual Learning}\label{learning}

The UIO estimate is not the final model; it is a robust teacher for learning the repeatable part of $\mu_x$. Decompose the generalized UIO output as
\begin{equation}
    \hat{\zeta}=\begin{bmatrix}\hat{\chi}_1&\hat{\chi}_2&\hat{x}_{\uio}&\hat{\mu}_x\end{bmatrix}^{\top}.
\end{equation}
A neural model $\mu_\theta=\mathcal N_\theta(\hat{x}_{\uio},\hat{\mu}_x,u)$ is inserted in a student observer
\begin{equation}\label{learning_uio_e2}
\dot{\hat{x}}_{\nn}=\varphi(\hat{x}_{\nn},u)+B\mu_\theta+\Lo_{\nn}(y-C\hat{x}_{\nn}).
\end{equation}
Writing the approximation error as $\Delta_x=\mu_x-\mu_\theta$, the student error $\epsilon_{\nn}=x-\hat{x}_{\nn}$ satisfies
\begin{equation}\label{learning_error}
\dot{\epsilon}_{\nn}=(A^x_j-\Lo_{\nn}C)\epsilon_{\nn}+B\Delta_x
\end{equation}
for each vertex $A^x_j$ enclosing the Jacobian of $\varphi$.

\begin{theorem}\label{ali_thm2}
If there exist $\mathcal P=\mathcal P^\top>0$, $\mathcal Q$, and $\sigma>0$ such that, for all $j$,
\begin{equation}\label{ali_LMI_2}
\begin{bmatrix}
(A^x_j)^\top\mathcal P+\mathcal PA^x_j-C^\top\mathcal Q-\mathcal Q^\top C
&\mathcal PB\\
B^\top\mathcal P&-\sigma I
\end{bmatrix}<0,
\end{equation}
then $\Lo_{\nn}=\mathcal P^{-1}\mathcal Q^\top$ gives
\[
\|\epsilon_{\nn}\|_{\mathcal L_2}\le \sqrt{\sigma}\|\Delta_x\|_{\mathcal L_2}
+\sqrt{\lambda_{\max}(\mathcal P)}\|\epsilon_{\nn}(0)\|.
\]
\end{theorem}

The network parameters are updated with a composite loss
\begin{equation}\label{loss_fun}
\begin{split}
\mathcal L(\theta)=&
\|y-C\hat{x}_{\nn}\|_{T_y}^2+
\|\hat{x}_{\nn}-\hat{x}_{\uio}\|_{T_u}^2\\
&+\lambda_\mu\|\mu_\theta-\hat{\mu}_x\|^2+
\lambda_\theta\|\theta\|^2 ,
\end{split}
\end{equation}
where the UIO consistency and residual terms prevent the student from drifting while the online learner adapts.

\section{Vehicle Teacher--Student Case Study}\label{vehicle_case}

The vehicle result is summarized with the state, input, and measurement vectors
\begin{equation}\label{vehicle_vectors}
\begin{aligned}
x&=[v_x\ v_y\ r\ \psi\ X\ Y]^\top,\\
u&=[a\ \delta]^\top,\qquad
y=[v_x\ r\ \psi\ X\ Y]^\top .
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
The discrete vehicle model is written as
\begin{equation}\label{eq_vehicle_discrete}
x_{k+1}=f_{\rm nom}(x_k,u_k)+B_{\rm tire}(x_k,u_k)w_k+G(x_k,u_k)d_k+\nu_k,
\end{equation}
where $w_k=[\Delta F_{yf,k}\ \Delta F_{yr,k}]^\top$ contains tire-only residual forces and $d_k$ collects the remaining mismatch. In continuous time, the tire residual channel is induced by
\begin{equation}\label{eq_btire_cont}
\begin{bmatrix}\dot v_x\\ \dot v_y\\ \dot r\end{bmatrix}
=f_{\rm nom,dyn}+
\begin{bmatrix}
0&0\\
1/m&1/m\\
l_f/I_z&-l_r/I_z
\end{bmatrix}
\begin{bmatrix}\Delta F_{yf}\\ \Delta F_{yr}\end{bmatrix}.
\end{equation}
Thus the teacher residual is physically interpretable and cannot become a generic error bucket.

Layer 1 is an augmented-state EKF/UIO teacher. With
$F_k=f_{\rm nom}(x_k,u_k)+B_{\rm tire}(x_k,u_k)w_k$,
\begin{equation}\label{teacher}
\chi_k=[x_k^\top\ w_k^\top]^\top,\qquad
\chi_{k+1}=[F_k^\top\ w_k^\top]^\top+q_k .
\end{equation}
The teacher is not influenced by the neural model. It gives $\hat{x}^{(1)}_{k|k}$ and $\hat{w}_{k|k}$ and forms the label
\begin{equation}\label{teacher_label}
r_k=S\left(\hat{x}^{(1)}_{k+1|k+1}-\hat{x}^{(1)}_{k+1|k}\right),
\end{equation}
where $S$ selects $(v_x,v_y,r)$. This label captures the correction left after nominal dynamics and tire residual reconstruction.

Layer 2 is the student estimator
\begin{equation}\label{student_pred}
\hat{x}^{(2)}_{k+1|k}=f_{\rm nom}(\hat{x}^{(2)}_{k|k},u_k)
B_{\rm tire}(\hat{x}^{(2)}_{k|k},u_k)\hat{w}_{k|k}
S^\top r_\theta(z_k),
\end{equation}
where $z_k=[\hat{x}^{(1)}_{k|k}\ u_k\ \rho_k\ {\rm flags}_k]^\top$. The online regression
\begin{equation}\label{ch4_nn_loss}
\min_\theta\sum_k \alpha_k\|r_\theta(z_k)-r_k\|^2
\end{equation}
uses gates $\alpha_k\in[0,1]$ to reject low-speed samples, yaw wrapping events, or large GNSS/IMU innovations.

In the simulation study from the merged chapter, the baseline physics-only observer tracks measured channels but shows larger lateral-state deviations under tire and parameter mismatch. The two-layer student reduces these deviations by injecting $r_\theta$, while the teacher keeps the tire-force reconstruction independent of the learned correction. Residual accumulation in the learning update smooths transient spikes, and the loss decreases during online training, confirming that the UIO-generated labels provide a usable supervision signal for the remaining mismatch.

\section{Conclusion}\label{sec7}

This paper condensed a regularized UIO, an output-derivative generalized UIO, and a UIO-supervised neural residual learner into a single IFAC-length framework. The merged vehicle case study separates tire-only residual estimation from neural learning of leftover mismatch. This preserves a stable physics-based teacher while allowing the student observer to improve prediction online. Future work will validate the method on real driving data and convert the continuous-time guarantees into a discrete-time implementation suitable for embedded deployment.
